Owning the site versus renting a builder

A rented builder is quick to start. Over time the invoice, the export limits, and a redesign you want to take with you become the real cost. A site you own is files, a host, and a person who can change them.

What you should be able to leave with

Your domain, your content, and a copy of the site you can host. Forms and analytics can stay with reputable tools. The pages stay in your files.

Owning the site still includes maintenance. DNS, certificates, content, and the occasional layout fix need an owner. The ongoing cost is the host and the time to change the site.

A sensible middle

Many British Columbia businesses we meet are paying builder fees for a brochure that barely changes. A static site plus a contact form covers that job. Larger sites with catalogues are a different conversation.
